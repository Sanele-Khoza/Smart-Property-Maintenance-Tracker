# SPMT Tenant Help Guide — Knowledge Base

*This document is written to be used as a reference source for an AI assistant answering tenant questions about the Smart Property Maintenance Tracker (SPMT) app. Each section is self-contained so a specific answer can be found and quoted without needing the rest of the document.*

---

## 1. What is SPMT?

SPMT (Smart Property Maintenance Tracker) is an app that lets tenants report maintenance problems in their unit — like a leaking pipe, a broken light, or a faulty air conditioner — without having to call or email the property manager. You take a photo, write a short description (or just attach a photo, a description isn't always required), and submit it. The system then automatically figures out what kind of problem it is, how urgent it is, and finds the right service provider to fix it — all without you needing to do anything else.

**Q: Is SPMT free to use?**
Yes — SPMT is provided by your property management at no extra cost to you as a tenant. There are no fees for submitting tickets or using the app.

**Q: Which devices can I use SPMT on?**
SPMT is a web-based app, so you can use it on any device with a modern web browser — a phone, tablet, laptop, or desktop computer. There is no separate app to download; just open your browser and go to the SPMT website.

**Q: Does SPMT work offline?**
SPMT requires an internet connection to submit tickets and view updates. If you lose connectivity, your form data may not be saved — always make sure you're connected before submitting.

## 2. Getting Started — Account & Login [TENANT]

**Q: How do I create an account?**
Open the app and select "Register." Enter your name, surname, email, phone number, and a password. Submit the form. Once your account is created, a property manager needs to approve you before you can log in — this is because tenants must be linked to a specific unit before they can use the app.

**Q: Why can't I log in yet after registering?**
New tenant accounts start in a "Pending" status. A property manager has to approve your account and assign you to your unit before you can submit tickets. If it's been a while, contact your property manager directly to check on this.

**Q: I forgot my password. What do I do?**
Use the "Forgot Password" link on the login screen. Enter your email, and you'll receive a link to reset it. This link expires after a set time, so use it soon after you receive it.

**Q: Can I change my email or phone number later?**
Yes, from your profile/account settings page once logged in. If you don't see this option yet, it may still be rolling out — contact your property manager if you need an urgent change.

**Q: Can I change my name on my account?**
Yes — go to your profile page after logging in and update your name and surname. If your property manager requires verification of the change, they may review it before approving.

**Q: Can I change my password after logging in?**
Yes — go to your profile or account settings and look for a "Change Password" option. You'll need to enter your current password first, then choose a new one. Choose a strong password with a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols.

**Q: What should I do if my account is locked?**
Your account can become locked after multiple failed login attempts — this is a security feature to protect you. To unlock it, use the "Forgot Password" link to reset your password. If that doesn't work, contact your property manager and they can unlock it for you.

**Q: Can I have more than one account?**
No — each tenant should have only one SPMT account linked to their email address. If you're having trouble with a duplicate account, contact your property manager to sort it out.

**Q: How do I deactivate or close my account?**
Contact your property manager to request account deactivation. They will review your request and handle the process — this ensures any open tickets linked to your account are dealt with properly. Your personal data will be handled in compliance with POPIA (South Africa's data protection law).

**Q: What happens to my tickets if I deactivate my account?**
Your ticket history is retained for record-keeping purposes, but you will no longer be able to log in or create new tickets. Your property manager can still view completed and historical ticket information.

**Q: How do I update my contact information (phone, email)?**
Go to your profile page after logging in. You can edit your phone number and email address there. If you change your email, you may need to verify the new address before it takes effect.

**Q: I'm not receiving the password reset email. What should I do?**
First check your spam/junk folder. Make sure you're using the exact email address you registered with. If you still don't receive it after a few minutes, contact your property manager — they can verify your account email and help you reset it manually.

**Q: What browser should I use for SPMT?**
SPMT works best on modern browsers like Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge, or Safari. Make sure your browser is up to date for the best experience. Internet Explorer is not supported.

## 3. Creating a Ticket (Reporting a Problem) [TENANT]

**Q: How do I create a ticket?**
1. Log in to the app.
2. Go to "Submit a Ticket" (or similar, on your tenant dashboard).
3. Add a short description of the problem, if you can — even a rough description helps.
4. Attach a photo of the issue if possible. This is genuinely important — the system uses the photo to help figure out what's wrong and how urgent it is.
5. Submit.

**Q: Do I have to write a description?**
No — you can submit with just a photo if that's easier. The system can still figure out the category from the image alone in many cases. That said, adding even a short description helps it be more accurate.

**Q: Can I choose what category my problem falls under (e.g. "Plumbing" or "Electrical")?**
No — and you don't need to. The system automatically figures out the category for you by analyzing your description and photo. You never have to know or guess what category your issue falls under.

**Q: What happens right after I submit a ticket?**
You'll get a ticket confirmation with a unique reference number. Behind the scenes, the system automatically:
- Reads your description and photo
- Figures out what kind of problem it is (plumbing, electrical, etc.)
- Decides how urgent it is
- Finds the best available service provider for the job
This all happens within seconds, with no action needed from you.

**Q: How does the system decide if something is an emergency?**
If your description or photo suggests something dangerous — like a gas smell, visible fire, flooding, or exposed sparking wires — the system automatically marks it as an Emergency and prioritizes it above all other tickets, regardless of what you selected. Emergency tickets get responded to fastest.

**Q: I described one problem but the photo shows something else. What happens?**
The system checks your description and photo independently. If they don't match up, it flags this so a property manager can take a closer look before anything is finalized — this is a safety feature, not an error on your part.

**Q: Can I submit more than one ticket for the same problem?**
You can, but it's better to add a comment or update to your existing ticket instead. Multiple tickets for the same issue can slow things down since the system may treat them as separate problems. If your original ticket hasn't been addressed, contact your property manager.

**Q: How many photos can I attach to a ticket?**
You can attach multiple photos to a single ticket. Adding several photos from different angles can help the service provider understand the problem better before arriving.

**Q: What file types are supported for photo uploads?**
JPEG and PNG images are supported. Make sure each photo is under 10MB. If you need to attach other file types, contact your property manager directly.

**Q: Can I submit a ticket on behalf of someone else (e.g. a neighbour)?**
Only the tenant linked to the affected unit can submit a ticket for that unit. If a neighbour has a maintenance issue, they should submit their own ticket, or you can ask your property manager to handle it on their behalf.

**Q: Can I attach a video to my ticket?**
Currently SPMT supports photo attachments only. If a video would help explain the issue, you can describe it in detail in the ticket description, or contact your property manager to arrange an alternative way to share the video.

**Q: What is a priority level and how is it set?**
Priority determines how urgently your ticket is handled. The system automatically sets the priority based on the type and severity of the issue. You don't need to set it yourself. Emergency issues get the highest priority, followed by High, Medium, and Low.

**Q: Can I set the priority of my ticket myself?**
No — the system determines priority automatically using AI analysis of your description and photo. This ensures that truly urgent issues are handled first, regardless of what anyone selects. If you feel the priority is wrong, contact your property manager.

**Q: Can I edit my ticket after submitting it?**
You can check your ticket's detail page for an edit option. Some fields may be locked once a provider has been assigned, since changing details mid-repair could cause confusion. Contact your property manager if you need to make significant changes.

**Q: Can I cancel a ticket?**
Contact your property manager if you need a ticket cancelled — this ensures nothing gets missed on their end.

**Q: I submitted a ticket by mistake. What do I do?**
Contact your property manager as soon as possible so they can cancel or close the ticket before a provider is dispatched. You can also check if the ticket has a cancel or delete option on its detail page.

**Q: What happens if I don't have a photo of the problem?**
You can still submit the ticket with just a description. The system will classify it based on your written description alone. However, adding a photo significantly improves the accuracy of the automatic classification and urgency detection.

## 4. Tracking Your Ticket [TENANT]

**Q: How do I check the status of a ticket I submitted?**
Go to your ticket list/dashboard — every ticket you've submitted is listed there with its current status.

**Q: What do the different statuses mean?**
- **Open** — submitted, not yet assigned to anyone.
- **Assigned** — a service provider has been linked to your ticket.
- **Accepted** — the provider has confirmed they'll do the job.
- **In Progress** — work has started.
- **Waiting for Parts** — the provider is waiting on materials/parts before continuing.
- **Completed** — the provider has finished the work.
- **Closed** — the ticket is fully resolved and confirmed by both you and the provider.
- **Reopened** — a previously closed ticket has been reopened (this requires a justification/reason to be given).

**Q: Can I edit or update a ticket after submitting it?**
Check your ticket's detail page for an edit option. Some fields may be locked once a provider has been assigned, since changing details mid-repair could cause confusion.

**Q: Can I cancel a ticket?**
Contact your property manager if you need a ticket cancelled — this ensures nothing gets missed on their end.

**Q: What does "Waiting for Parts" mean?**
This means the assigned provider has started the job but needs specific parts or materials to complete the repair. The provider will continue once the parts arrive. You don't need to do anything — the system tracks this status automatically.

**Q: What does "Escalated" mean?**
An escalated ticket has been flagged for special attention — this could be because it's overdue, involves a safety concern, or the provider encountered something unexpected. A property manager will review it and take appropriate action.

**Q: What does "On Hold" mean?**
An "On Hold" ticket is temporarily paused. This might happen if the provider needs more information from you, or if external factors are delaying the work. The provider or property manager will resume it when ready.

**Q: What does "Manual Review" mean?**
If the AI system is unsure about the category, urgency, or there's a conflict between the description and photo, the ticket goes into manual review. A property manager will examine it and make the final classification before assigning a provider.

**Q: Can I reopen a closed ticket?**
Yes — if the same problem comes back after a ticket was closed, you can reopen it. You'll need to provide a reason for reopening. Go to the ticket detail and look for the "Reopen" option. Note that reopening requires a valid justification.

**Q: How long does it typically take for a ticket to be resolved?**
Resolution times depend on the priority and complexity of the issue. Emergency tickets are handled first. The system sets expected response and resolution deadlines for each ticket based on its priority. You can check the SLA deadline on the ticket detail page.

**Q: Will I be notified when my ticket status changes?**
Yes — you'll receive a notification each time your ticket moves to a new status (Assigned, In Progress, Completed, etc.). Make sure your notification preferences are enabled in your profile settings.

**Q: Can I see the acceptance note my provider left?**
Yes — when a provider accepts your ticket, they can leave a message for you. This message is visible in your ticket detail under the "Message from provider" section in the Ticket Details card.

**Q: How do I check the audit trail of my ticket?**
Each ticket has an Audit Trail section on its detail page. This shows a timeline of all status changes, who made them, and any notes or comments associated with each change.

## 5. Service Providers [TENANT]

**Q: How does the system pick which provider fixes my problem?**
The system ranks available providers by three things: their rating from other tenants, how close they are to your property, and how busy they currently are. It automatically recommends the best match — you don't need to search for or choose a provider yourself.

**Q: Can I choose my own provider?**
No — providers are matched automatically to keep the process fair and fast for everyone. If you have a concern about who was assigned, speak to your property manager.

**Q: What if the assigned provider doesn't show up or seems wrong for the job?**
Contact your property manager — they can review the assignment and make a change if needed.

**Q: Can I request a specific provider for future tickets?**
While you can't directly choose a provider, you can mention a preference in your ticket description or speak to your property manager. Final assignment decisions rest with the system and the property manager to ensure fairness.

**Q: What does it mean when a provider is "Available" versus "Busy"?**
"Available" means the provider currently has capacity to take on new jobs. "Busy" means they are actively working on other tickets and may take longer to respond. The system considers workload when matching providers.

**Q: What happens if the provider damages something during the repair?**
Contact your property manager immediately with details and photos of any damage. The property manager will work with the provider and management to resolve the situation appropriately.

**Q: Will the provider contact me before arriving?**
Provider communication varies. Check the acceptance note on your ticket for any messages from the provider. If you need to arrange a specific time, contact your property manager to coordinate.

**Q: Can the provider enter my unit without me being home?**
This depends on your property's policies. Discuss access arrangements with your property manager. For emergency situations (e.g., flooding, gas leak), providers may need immediate access to prevent further damage.

## 6. After the Work Is Done [TENANT]

**Q: How do I rate a service provider?**
Once your ticket is marked "Closed," go to that ticket and you'll be able to leave a rating (1 to 5 stars) and an optional comment. Ratings help future tenants and also affect how often that provider gets matched to new jobs.

**Q: Can I rate a provider before the ticket is closed?**
No — ratings can only be submitted after the ticket is fully closed, to make sure the rating reflects completed work.

**Q: I already rated this ticket, can I change my rating?**
No — each ticket can only be rated once, to keep the rating system fair and accurate.

**Q: Why are my ratings important?**
Ratings directly affect how the system matches providers to future jobs. Highly rated providers get matched more often, which means better service for all tenants. Your honest feedback helps keep the system fair and effective.

**Q: What if the work wasn't done properly?**
If you're not satisfied with the repair, contact your property manager. They can review the situation and arrange for the work to be redone or escalated. Don't close or confirm the ticket if the issue isn't resolved.

**Q: Can I leave a comment with my rating?**
Yes — when you rate a provider, you can optionally add a comment about your experience. This feedback is visible to property managers and helps them maintain service quality.

**Q: What happens after I confirm the ticket is complete?**
Once you confirm the work is done ("Tenant Confirmed"), the ticket moves to "Closed." At that point you can leave a rating. The provider's performance metrics are updated based on your feedback.

## 7. Notifications [TENANT]

**Q: How will I know when something happens with my ticket?**
You'll receive notifications for major events — when your ticket is assigned, when work starts, when it's marked complete, and if it's ever escalated. Check your notification preferences in your profile settings to control how you're notified.

**Q: My ticket seems to be taking a long time. Will I be notified if it's overdue?**
Yes — every ticket has an expected response and resolution time based on its priority. If it's approaching that deadline, the system automatically alerts your property manager to follow up.

**Q: What types of notifications will I receive?**
You'll receive notifications when: your ticket is assigned to a provider, the provider accepts the job, work begins, the ticket is put on hold or escalated, the work is marked complete, and when you need to confirm the work or rate the provider.

**Q: How do I turn notifications on or off?**
Go to your profile settings and look for notification preferences. You can control which notifications you receive and how (in-app, email, or push). It's recommended to keep ticket-related notifications enabled so you don't miss updates.

**Q: I'm not getting email notifications. What's wrong?**
Check your spam or junk folder first. Make sure your email address in your profile is correct. Also verify that email notifications are enabled in your notification preferences. If the problem persists, contact your property manager.

**Q: Will I get notifications if I'm not logged in?**
In-app notifications only appear when you're logged in. If you've enabled email notifications, those will be sent regardless. Make sure your notification preferences include the channels you want to use.

**Q: Can I get SMS notifications?**
SMS notifications may be available depending on your property manager's settings. Check your notification preferences for an SMS option. If it's not available, contact your property manager to ask about enabling it.

## 8. Safety

**Q: What should I do if I have a genuine emergency right now (gas leak, fire, flooding)?**
Submit a ticket immediately with as much detail as possible — the system will detect emergency keywords/imagery and prioritize it automatically. However, if there is immediate danger to life or property, contact emergency services directly first — do not wait on the app for a life-threatening situation.

**Q: When should I call emergency services instead of using SPMT?**
Call emergency services (10111 for police, 10177 for ambulance/fire) immediately if there's a fire, gas leak, flood causing structural risk, electrical hazard, break-in, or any situation threatening life or safety. SPMT is for maintenance issues, not life-threatening emergencies.

**Q: What counts as an emergency in SPMT?**
SPMT considers these as emergencies: gas smells or leaks, visible fire or smoke, active flooding or water damage, exposed electrical wiring, burst pipes, structural damage that makes the unit unsafe, and any situation where health or safety is at immediate risk.

**Q: Will an emergency ticket get faster service?**
Yes — emergency tickets are prioritized above all other tickets. The system will dispatch a provider as quickly as possible and escalate to the property manager immediately.

**Q: What if I smell gas in my unit?**
Leave the unit immediately, do not use any electrical switches or flames, and call the emergency gas services number or your property manager right away. Once you're safe, submit an SPMT ticket describing the gas smell — the system will treat it as an emergency.

## 9. Your Data & Privacy

**Q: Is my personal information safe?**
Yes — your account details are protected with industry-standard security (encrypted passwords, secure login sessions), and personal data is stored in compliance with South Africa's data protection law (POPIA).

**Q: Who can see my ticket details and photos?**
Only you, your property manager, the assigned service provider, and system administrators can see your ticket information — no other tenants can see your tickets or personal details.

**Q: How long is my data kept?**
Your account data and ticket history are retained for as long as your account is active and for a reasonable period afterwards for record-keeping. If you deactivate your account, your data is handled in accordance with POPIA.

**Q: Can I request a copy of my personal data?**
Under POPIA, you have the right to request access to your personal data. Contact your property manager to make a data access request.

**Q: Can I request that my data be deleted?**
Under POPIA, you have the right to request deletion of your personal data in certain circumstances. Contact your property manager to discuss this. Note that some data may need to be retained for legal or record-keeping purposes.

**Q: Who owns the photos I upload?**
The photos you upload are associated with your ticket for the purpose of maintenance resolution. They are used by the AI system to classify your issue and by the provider to prepare for the repair. They are not shared publicly.

**Q: Is my data shared with third parties?**
Your data is used within the SPMT system for maintenance management purposes only. It is not sold or shared with third-party advertisers. Limited data may be shared with service providers solely for the purpose of resolving your maintenance issue.

**Q: Where is my data stored?**
Your data is stored on secure servers. The system uses encryption and security best practices to protect your information.

## 10. Troubleshooting

**Q: My photo won't upload.**
Check the file is a JPEG or PNG image and under the maximum allowed size (typically 10MB). Try a different photo or a smaller file size if it keeps failing.

**Q: I'm not receiving any notifications.**
Check your notification preferences in your profile settings, and check your email's spam folder if you're expecting an email notification.

**Q: The app says my account is locked.**
This happens automatically after several failed login attempts, as a security measure. Use the password reset option, or contact your property manager if the issue continues.

**Q: The app is loading very slowly.**
Check your internet connection first. Try clearing your browser cache and reloading. If the problem persists, it could be a temporary server issue — try again in a few minutes.

**Q: I get an error message when submitting a ticket.**
Make sure all required fields are filled in and your photos are valid (JPEG/PNG, under 10MB). If the error continues, note down the error message and contact your property manager.

**Q: My ticket disappeared from my dashboard.**
This could happen if the ticket was moved to trash by mistake, or if there was a filter applied to the list. Check the trash section or try removing any active filters. If you still can't find it, contact your property manager.

**Q: The app looks different or some features are missing.**
The app may have been recently updated. Some features are still being rolled out. If something you need is missing, contact your property manager for assistance.

**Q: I can't see my property or unit information.**
This means your account may not be linked to a property yet. Contact your property manager to assign you to the correct property and unit.

**Q: I get logged out unexpectedly.**
This can happen if your session expires (sessions time out after a period of inactivity for security). Simply log in again. If it happens frequently, check that cookies are enabled in your browser.

**Q: The app isn't working on my phone's browser.**
Try updating your browser to the latest version. If that doesn't work, try a different browser (Chrome, Firefox, or Edge). Make sure JavaScript is enabled — SPMT requires it.

## 13. Tickets, Priority & SLA

**Q: What is an SLA?**
SLA stands for "Service Level Agreement." It's the expected timeframe within which your ticket should be responded to and resolved. The system sets SLA deadlines automatically based on the priority of your ticket.

**Q: What are the SLA response and resolution times?**
Response time is how quickly a provider should acknowledge your ticket. Resolution time is how quickly the issue should be fully fixed. These times depend on the priority level — Emergency tickets have the shortest deadlines, Low priority tickets have the longest.

**Q: What happens if my ticket is overdue?**
If a ticket passes its SLA deadline, it's automatically flagged as "SLA Breached." The property manager is notified and can take action to expedite the resolution.

**Q: Can the SLA deadline be extended?**
The property manager can adjust SLA deadlines in exceptional circumstances — for example, if parts are on order or the issue is complex. Contact your property manager if you have concerns about a deadline.

**Q: What is the difference between "Completed" and "Closed"?**
"Completed" means the provider has finished the work. "Closed" means you have confirmed the work is done (or the ticket has been automatically closed). Only after closure can you leave a rating.

## 15. Property Manager Guide [PROPERTY_MANAGER]

**Q: How do I approve a new tenant account?**
Open the Tenants page from your sidebar. Any tenant in "Pending" status appears there in a list. Select the tenant and choose to approve them, then link them to the correct property and unit. Until they are approved and linked, they cannot log in or submit tickets.

**Q: How do I assign a ticket to a service provider?**
Go to the Tickets page and open the ticket you want to assign. Use the assignment panel to pick a service provider. You can also let the system auto-assign based on rating, proximity, and current workload. Once assigned, the provider is notified and can accept or decline.

**Q: How does the AI review workflow work?**
Tickets with a conflict between the description and photo, or a low-confidence classification, go into manual review. Open the AI Review page to see these tickets, review the AI's labels and confidence, correct the classification if needed, and then assign the ticket. This is a safety check, not a fault on your part.

**Q: How do I escalate a ticket?**
Open the ticket and use the escalation option. Escalation flags the ticket for special attention — useful when it is overdue, involves a safety concern, or the provider ran into something unexpected. The ticket is given a higher priority and highlighted across dashboards.

**Q: How do I run a reports or performance report?**
Open the Reports page. You can filter by date range, property, category, and provider. The page shows SLA compliance, resolution times, priority breakdowns, and provider performance, and you can export the data. Use these reports to spot overdue or frequently-breached priorities.

**Q: How do I add or edit a service provider/technician?**
Open the Technicians page. You can add a new provider by selecting a registered service-provider account and linking them, edit their specialties, and adjust their status. Providers must be active to receive assignments.

**Q: What can I do about an SLA breach?**
When a ticket breaches its SLA deadline it is flagged automatically and you are notified. Open the ticket, review why it is overdue, and take action — reassign the provider, escalate the ticket, or extend the SLA deadline in exceptional circumstances such as parts on order.

**Q: How do I manage properties and units?**
Use the Properties and Units pages to add, edit, or deactivate properties and their units. Tenants are linked to units, so make sure a tenant's unit assignment is correct when you approve them.

**Q: How do I review a provider's completed work?**
When a provider marks a job complete, it appears in your ticket list ready for review. Confirm the work was done properly before closing the ticket, and address any tenant complaints by reopening or arranging for a redo.

**Q: What is the difference between completing and closing a ticket?**
"Completed" means the provider has finished the work. "Closed" means you (or the tenant) have confirmed the work is done. Only after closure can a rating be submitted. If the tenant isn't satisfied, keep the ticket open and escalate.

**Q: Can I edit a ticket after it has been assigned?**
Yes, property managers can edit ticket details even after assignment. Changing details mid-repair can cause confusion, so review any tenant edit requests and make the changes when appropriate. You can also postpone tickets with a reason.

**Q: How do I handle a tenant who reports the same problem repeatedly?**
Check whether the tenant is submitting duplicate tickets for the same issue. Ask them to update the original ticket instead, or combine duplicates using the duplicate-group tools. If a resolved problem has come back, the tenant can reopen the original ticket with a reason.

**Q: How do I notify tenants about scheduled work?**
Use the Messages section to broadcast an announcement, or send a direct message to a tenant. Ticket status changes trigger automatic notifications, so tenants are kept informed without extra work on your side.

**Q: Who else can see the property manager's dashboards and reports?**
Your property data and reports are visible to system administrators as well. Tenants and service providers can only see their own tickets and relevant assignments — they do not see your management dashboards.

## 16. Service Provider Guide [SERVICE_PROVIDER]

**Q: How do I view and accept jobs assigned to me?**
Open My Jobs from your sidebar. Jobs assigned to you appear there with their status and details. Use the accept action to confirm you will do the job — you can also decline if you genuinely cannot, though declining often triggers a re-assignment notification to the property manager.

**Q: How do I update the status of a job?**
You can move a job through Accepted, In Progress, Waiting for Parts, and Completed. Use the Job Detail page for the job and select the new status. When you start a job, mark it In Progress; when finished, submit completion with any notes.

**Q: What should I do when I need parts or materials?**
Set the job to "Waiting for Parts". This tells the tenant and property manager you are ready to continue as soon as the parts arrive, and the system tracks this status automatically. You do not need to keep the ticket open for anything else.

**Q: How do I handle an emergency job?**
Emergency jobs are flagged at the top of your dashboard and appear on the Emergency page. They take priority over everything else. Respond as quickly as possible and follow the safety guidance on the ticket. If the situation is life-threatening, tell the tenant to contact emergency services directly.

**Q: How do I leave a message for the tenant when I accept a job?**
When accepting a job you can add a note. This note is shown to the tenant in the "Message from provider" section of the ticket details, e.g., an estimated arrival time. You can also coordinate access times this way.

**Q: How is my performance tracked?**
The My Performance page shows your rating, completed jobs, and response times. Your rating comes from tenant ratings after a ticket is closed, and it directly affects how often the system matches you to new jobs — higher-rated providers get matched more often.

**Q: Can I schedule my own jobs?**
The Schedule page shows your upcoming work. Scheduling is largely managed by the system and property manager based on urgency, but you can view your workload and upcoming visits to plan your day.

**Q: How do I handle a ticket I cannot complete?**
If you cannot complete a job, inform the property manager so they can re-assign or escalate it. Do not leave a job stuck in a partial status — keep the ticket moving so SLA deadlines are not breached.

**Q: What should I do if the tenant reports the work was not done properly?**
If a job is reopened or the tenant is unsatisfied, coordinate with the property manager to arrange a redo. Your rating reflects the final outcome, so completing jobs properly the first time protects your performance score.

**Q: How do I know where the property is located?**
The ticket details include the property and unit information. Some tickets may include GPS location of the provider or property to help you navigate. Follow any access instructions in the ticket or arrange a time with the tenant through the property manager.

## 17. System Administrator Guide [SYSTEM_ADMIN]

**Q: How do I manage users and accounts?**
Open the Users page. You can view, suspend, deactivate, or permanently delete user accounts, change roles, search by name or email, and see each account's status and activity. Use the filter and search tools for large lists.

**Q: How do I approve a service provider?**
A service provider account starts in a pending state after registration. From the Users page, find the pending provider and approve them. Only approved providers appear on the Technicians list and are eligible to receive assignments.

**Q: How do I manage system settings?**
Open the Settings page. There you can configure AI confidence thresholds, SLA minutes per priority, notification templates, and system-wide preferences. Changes apply immediately across the app.

**Q: How do I create a backup?**
Open the Backup page and select Create Backup. The system exports the current database contents (users, properties, units, tickets, audit logs) to a downloadable file. You can also restore from a previous backup or download a data export.

**Q: How do I check system health?**
Open the System Health page. It shows the status of backend services, authentication, database connectivity, and connected adapters like the AI classifier and chatbot. Any failing service appears in red so you can act quickly.

**Q: How do I view activity and audit logs?**
Open Audit Logs to see a full, timestamped history of actions across the system — who did what and when. The Activity page shows recent events on the dashboard. These logs help with compliance and debugging.

**Q: How do I configure roles and permissions?**
Open the Roles page. You can view each role's permissions and edit which permissions a role has by toggling them. Role changes affect what users can see and do throughout the app.

**Q: How do I manage notifications and messages globally?**
Open the Notifications page to see delivery statuses and templates, and the Messages page for in-app messaging. You can also view logs about messages and understand delivery chains from there.

**Q: How do I handle an SLA breach across the platform?**
Open the Reports or Analytics dashboard to see SLA compliance across all priorities. If breaches spike, review the affected properties and providers, and use the overview to see open vs completed tickets and where workloads are heavy.

**Q: How do I integrate or check the AI services?**
System Health shows the AI classifier and chatbot. If the chatbot is offline, restart it from the ai-chatbot folder (`python server.py`). If the Python AI service is down, start it with the standard uvicorn command for the AI service. Re-run health checks after restarting.

**Q: Who can approve tenant accounts?**
Property managers approve and link tenant accounts to units. As a system administrator you can also approve accounts if needed. Once approved and linked, tenants can log in and submit tickets.

**Q: Can I change a user's role?**
Yes — from the Users page you can change a user's role (e.g., a tenant to a property manager). Role changes immediately affect their access and dashboard. Only grant elevated roles (administrator) to trusted users.

**Q: What happens when I deactivate a user account?**
The user is logged out immediately and can no longer log in or take actions. Their data and ticket history are retained. Suspension is a temporary state that can be reversed; deactivation is a longer-term action.
